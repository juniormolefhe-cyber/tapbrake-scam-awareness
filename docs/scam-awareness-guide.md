# Scam Awareness Guide

This guide explains common phone and mobile money scams in Botswana. It is for anyone who wants to recognise a scam before tapping a link, sharing a code, or sending money.

## Fake bank messages

A scam SMS may say your account is blocked or that your banking app is out of date. It asks you to tap a link and enter your banking details. Botswana Police warned about messages like this in 2025 (Botswana Police Service, 2025).

## Callers asking for codes

Someone may call and claim to be from your bank or mobile money provider. They may ask you to read out a one-time code sent to your phone. That code can give them access to your account (SchoolsFirst FCU, n.d.).

## Prize and refund scams

A caller or message may say you have won a prize, but you must first pay a fee. Real prizes do not require you to pay money first (Bank of Botswana, 2025).

## SIM swaps

A criminal may convince a mobile network to move your phone number to a new SIM card. Your calls and one-time codes may then go to the criminal instead of you (NatWest International, n.d.).

## What to do

If you think you have been targeted, stop replying, call your bank or mobile money provider using an official number, change your PIN, keep the messages as evidence, and report the incident to the police on 999.
