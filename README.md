# Tapbrake

**Brake before you tap.**

Tapbrake is a social engineering awareness campaign about phone and mobile money scams in Botswana. It helps people pause before they tap a suspicious link, share a PIN, or give away a one-time code.

The campaign is built around one simple idea: scams work in the second between reading a message and tapping it. Tapbrake encourages people to stop, check, and protect their money.

## What Tapbrake is

Tapbrake is an independent awareness project. It is not linked to any bank, mobile network, or government office.

It explains common scams such as fake bank SMS messages, callers asking for one-time codes, prize scams, and SIM swaps. It also gives simple actions people can take if they have already tapped a link or shared information.

The message is practical: **take one, share one.** If one person learns to pause before tapping, they can help protect someone else too.

## Who it’s for

Tapbrake is for anyone in Botswana who uses a phone, mobile money, or banking services.

It is especially useful for:

- Older people and pensioners who may be targeted by fraudsters.
- Young people who can help family members understand scam messages.
- Community groups, schools, churches, and workplaces that share safety information.
- Anyone who wants a simple way to explain phone scams to relatives or neighbours.

The campaign recognises that people do not fall for scams because they are foolish. Many messages are designed to create panic, urgency, or hope, especially when someone is busy or worried.

## How to use it

1. Open the `brochure/` folder to read or download the main Tapbrake brochure.
2. Use the `checklists/` folder when you need a quick response plan, especially if someone has already tapped a link or shared a code.
3. Use the `docs/` folder to learn more about phishing, social engineering, prize scams, and SIM swaps.
4. Use the `templates/` folder to adapt the campaign for a family group, community meeting, school, or workplace.
5. Share the brochure with at least one person each week. Read it aloud for anyone who finds small print difficult.

The core habit is **BRAKE**:

- **B — Breathe.** Do not act immediately.
- **R — Read it again.** Check who sent it and what they are asking for.
- **A — Ask someone.** Show the message to a person you trust.
- **K — Keep your codes.** Never share PINs, passwords, or one-time codes.
- **E — Exit and check.** Close the message and contact your bank or mobile money provider using an official number.

## Reporting safely

If you think you have been scammed, act quickly but do not panic.

1. Stop talking to the caller and stop replying to the message.
2. Hang up or close the message.
3. Call your bank or mobile money provider using the number on your card or in the official app. Ask them to block or secure your account.
4. Change your PIN and passwords from a device you trust.
5. Keep the SMS, call log, and any screenshots as evidence.
6. Report the incident to the Botswana Police Service on **999** or at your nearest police station.
7. Tell family or friends so they can watch out for the same scam.

If your phone suddenly loses signal for no clear reason, call your mobile network from another phone. This can be a sign of a SIM swap.
